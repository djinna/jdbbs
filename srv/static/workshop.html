Protocolize Your Book

Run your manuscript through a book-production factory · with Jenna Dixon, jdbb studio

"Protocol" workshops can get abstract — this one is a DIY factory. Bring a real manuscript (a half-finished book, a pile of essays, a thread archive, a zine) and over four sessions we push it through a protocolized production pipeline: a transmittal handshake that generates a custom authoring template, a conformance preflight that negotiates messy human input into machine-readable structure, and a deterministic transform that yields a print PDF and an EPUB from one source. You handle the protocol concepts hands-on — typed interfaces, controlled vocabularies, inheritable templates — and delegate some drafting to an LLM agent to see why a human-legible protocol is also machine-legible. Everyone leaves with a typeset artifact and a felt answer to the symposium's question: how much of a craft should be protocolized, and what does it cost?

Who it's for

Writers, editors, designers, small-press and self-publishers, zine-makers, newsletter authors, and protocol-curious people who have a body of text they'd like to see become a real book. No coding, typesetting, or protocol-studies background required — the factory handles the machinery; you make the protocol decisions.

One hard requirement

You must bring real material — a manuscript or text collection you'd like to turn into a book. Any size, rough is welcome. This is non-negotiable: the workshop runs on your files, not samples.

You'll leave with

  • A typeset PDF + EPUB of your own material, produced through the factory.
  • A reusable custom authoring template generated from your transmittal.
  • A transferable mental model: your craft re-seen as a protocol stack — typed interfaces, controlled vocabularies, inheritable templates.
  • A decision rubric for what to protocolize and what to leave to craft judgment — useful well beyond book production.
  • First-hand experience delegating creative work to an LLM agent, and a sense of why it works.
