Editorial Stylesheet — Protocolized Anthology (working draft)

A living reference for copy editing the stories in this series. Goal: consistency across stories while preserving each author's voice. This is a light copy edit regime — fix mechanics, don't rewrite. When in doubt, leave the author's choice.

Started from: 01 Aster's Story: Presence Must Preserve

0. Workflow (how we process each story)

  1. Author/editor exports the Google Doc as Microsoft Word (.docx) (keeps italics, smart quotes, em dashes, scene breaks). Plain-text paste loses these.
  2. Drop the file in editorial/Zoothesia/source/NN Title.docx.
  3. From editorial/Zoothesia/, extract for editing without losing emphasis: pandoc -f docx -t markdown --wrap=none "source/NN Title.docx" -o work.md
  4. Edit work.md (asterisks = the author's italics; verify each one is real).
  5. Render back: pandoc -f markdown -t docx work.md -o "edited/NN Title (copyedited).docx"
  6. Deliver clean edited .docx + a NN-...-CHANGELOG.md (before → after list plus author queries). We do clean edits, not tracked-changes redlines (pandoc can't author Word revision marks reliably); the changelog is the review surface.
  7. Sanity-check emphasis survived the round-trip: pandoc -f docx -t markdown edited/....docx | grep -oP '\*[^*]+\*'

Italic-export gotcha: Google Docs sometimes extends an italic run over the trailing ." of a sentence. After extraction, check that closing punctuation isn't accidentally inside *...*; move it out. (Happened once in Story 01.)

Literal asterisks: if the author types *gag* as characters (not as an italic style), pandoc shows it escaped as \*gag\*. Preserve that — it's an authorial choice, not emphasis.

2. Voice & Register (preserve, don't sanitize)

These stories use a casual, interior first-person voice. Keep:

  • Sentence-fragments used for rhythm/emphasis. "Poof. There." / "Or dangerous."
  • Conversational discourse markers: "Like, sure,…", "I mean it", "I guess", "sure".
  • Profanity and slang in dialogue and narration (do not soften).
  • Coined/neologism vocabulary (see Lexicon). Do not “correct” invented words.
  • Intentional repetition for effect (e.g., "The same… The same…").

Fix: grammar slips, missing/extra words, agreement errors, typos, mistaken homophones, dropped punctuation, inconsistent styling.

When a fragment vs. error is ambiguous, preserve if it scans as deliberate.

3. Dialogue Formatting

  • Each speaker gets a new paragraph.
  • Put a Q&A exchange on separate lines/paragraphs, even when terse ("You've got a good job?" / "Why do you care?").
  • Dialogue tags lowercase after a comma: she said, "Let's go." and "Never," he said.
  • Capitalize the first word of quoted dialogue introduced by a comma: shrugged and said, "Let's go."

4. Formatting / Typography

  • Titles of in-world media, tracks, filters, products: italics. Marine Serenity: Safe Waters in Your Social Seas; Pink Sunrises.
  • Editorial/sound asides the author types with literal asterisks (e.g. *gag*): keep the literal asterisks — do not convert to italics. Place sentence punctuation logically (in Story 01: (... \*gag\*.) — period inside the paren).
  • Emphasis vs. punctuation: ensure trailing ./,/" are outside an italic span unless the author clearly intended otherwise.
  • Story header banner kept verbatim as delivered: = = = NN Title: Subtitle = = =
  • Scene breaks: use a blank line or * * * consistently (TBD per layout; the DOCX/Typst pipeline handles ---).

6. Recurring Mechanical Fixes (watch-list)

These were the actual corrections in Aster's Story; expect similar in others:

  • Hyphenation of compound modifiers: taped-off, skin-deep, drug-fueled, ivory-colored, sickly sweetness (open as noun).
  • Possessives: someone’s, others’ — check apostrophe placement.
  • its vs. it’s: "its faultless humanity" (possessive), not "it's."
  • angrier not "angier"; desiccated not "dessicated"; putrefying not "putrifying"; hall pass two words.
  • Subject/verb & there was/were: "there were so many more people."
  • Dropped/duplicated words: delete false starts like "I was the I couldn't…".
  • Comma before terminal "sometimes"/parentheticals — verify.
  • "step up to the corner" (missing preposition).
  • Question marks on questions phrased as statements in interior monologue ("To judge the lives of those whose urges were completely alien?").
  • Plurals vs. names: "shouted out the names of the body parts."

7. What NOT to change

  • Authorial fragments and run-ons used stylistically.
  • Slang, vulgarity, and dialect in dialogue.
  • Invented terminology and neologisms (style them per the Lexicon).
  • Voice-driven comma splices in fast interior passages — light edit only; query the author rather than silently rewriting if meaning is at stake.

8. Author Queries (log per story)

Raise (don't silently fix) when:

  • A coined term is spelled inconsistently and you can't tell which is canonical.
  • A sentence's meaning is genuinely ambiguous.
  • A factual/continuity detail conflicts within or across stories (e.g., timeline, place names, the rules of the Zoo).
